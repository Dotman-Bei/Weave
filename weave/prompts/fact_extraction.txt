Extract the durable entities and first-person facts stated in one turn of a chat
conversation. This feeds a long-term memory graph, so precision matters more
than volume: record what the speaker asserts about themselves and their world,
not what they are merely asking about or considering.

Rules:
- Only record facts the {speaker} asserts as true. Ignore questions,
  hypotheticals, and suggestions made by the assistant.
- The subject of a first-person fact is always "user".
- Predicates are lowercase snake_case, and are specialised by the category of
  the object so that unrelated facts never collide. Prefer forms like
  prefers_language, likes_beverage, lives_in, works_at, favorite_color,
  allergic_to, uses_tool, has_pet.
- Objects are short noun phrases in lowercase, with no articles.
- Use "qualifier" for a purpose or scope attached to the fact, e.g. "I prefer Go
  for data pipelines" has object "go" and qualifier "data pipelines".
- Set polarity to "negative" for statements of dislike, avoidance or negation.
- Copy the sentence the fact came from into "evidence" verbatim.
- Return empty lists when the turn states nothing durable. Small talk,
  acknowledgements and questions produce nothing.
